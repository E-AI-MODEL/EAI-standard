# Direct Instruction adapter: Engelmann tradition

Status: candidate

This adapter represents **Direct Instruction (DI)** in the Engelmann tradition as a distinct model family member. It must not be treated as a synonym for generic direct instruction, Explicit Instruction, or EDI.

## Source basis

The candidate adapter uses current public materials from the National Institute for Direct Instruction (NIFDI), including its model philosophy, authentic-program characteristics, mastery guidance and implementation descriptions.

Important public sources include:

- NIFDI, *Basic Philosophy of Direct Instruction*;
- NIFDI, *Authentic Direct Instruction* program features;
- NIFDI, *Teaching to Mastery*;
- NIFDI, *Focusing on Student Performance*;
- NIFDI, *DI vs. di*.

## Structural difference from the other adapters

Unlike the Archer-Hughes and EDI adapters, this candidate does not force DI into a generic phase sequence. Public DI sources describe a tightly designed curricular and instructional system with program design, placement, grouping, scripted presentation, response systems, correction, mastery checks and progress monitoring.

That structure is preserved as model elements and relations. Where a specific DI program has its own lesson sequence, that sequence should be represented in a program-specific child adapter rather than invented at family level.

## Why this matters for EAI

DI is a useful stress test because professional action is distributed differently than in a loosely specified teaching approach. Some design decisions are already embedded in validated curricular materials, while teachers still observe responses, correct errors, monitor mastery, pace delivery and respond to student performance.

AI allocation therefore cannot be judged simply by asking whether the teacher or AI 'designed the lesson'. The relevant professional action may instead be accurate placement, observation, correction, mastery interpretation or adaptation within the model's constraints.
