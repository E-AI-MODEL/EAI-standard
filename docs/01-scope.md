# Scope

## In scope

The EAI Standard describes:

- human roles in AI-supported educational situations;
- context-dependent core human actions;
- decomposition into microstructures;
- human-AI task allocation;
- evidence of human performance, judgement and learning;
- handback of relevant actions to the human;
- remediation and renewed demonstration;
- model adapters for existing didactic and pedagogical approaches;
- context overlays such as subject, level, age or curriculum.

## Out of scope

The standard does not:

- select a preferred teaching or pedagogical model;
- prescribe lesson phases;
- rank AI products or vendors;
- define legal compliance;
- replace professional safeguarding procedures;
- define curriculum content;
- infer learning from product quality alone;
- assign one universal acceptable percentage of AI use.

## Unit of analysis

The preferred unit of analysis is a concrete situation small enough that the relevant human action can be identified.

Examples:

- a learner formulates an argument during guided practice;
- a teacher interprets an error pattern before choosing feedback;
- a mentor responds to a learner expressing disengagement;
- a learner evaluates the reliability of a source during research;
- a teacher decides whether a learner is ready for independent practice.

Whole lessons or assignments may be analysed, but they should be decomposed when different actions, actors or AI roles occur within them.

## Actor scope

The current candidate standard covers two primary human actor classes:

- `teacher_professional`
- `learner`

`multiple_humans` is available where a case needs to represent a bounded shared human actor grouping without collapsing teacher/professional and learner actions that are materially distinct.

Additional roles may be added later where the educational context requires them, but they should not duplicate an existing role without a clear semantic reason.
